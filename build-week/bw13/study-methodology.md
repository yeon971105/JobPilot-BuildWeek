# Directional usability study methodology

## Design

Five consenting adults completed a moderated comparison using two synthetic roles: Analytics Operations Engineer and Data Enablement Engineer. Every participant reviewed a traditional posting first and the JobPilot presentation second. Assignment groups counterbalanced which role appeared in each condition, and each participant saw a different role per condition to prevent same-role carryover.

Condition order was not counterbalanced. Practice or order effects may contribute to the observed difference, so the evidence is directional rather than causal.

## Measures

- Completion time for each condition.
- Four factual questions: required experience, preferred experience, work arrangement, and weakest-supported qualification.
- Apply / Review Further / Skip decision alignment against a frozen reference. A different decision was not labeled incorrect.
- Self-reported confidence and clarity on seven-point scales.

## Collection and privacy

The study used browser-local anonymous collection under `jobpilot-decision-utility.v2`. It collected no name, email, phone number, resume, demographics, employment status, health information, home address, or exported IP address. Five Final exports passed the validator; Preview, Pilot, dry-run, browser-test, incomplete, duplicate, and malformed records remained excluded.

Participant CSVs and the combined dataset remain local and untracked. Public evidence contains aggregate values and the frozen combined-file hash only.

## Interpretation boundary

The sample is small and fixed. No confidence interval or statistical-significance test is reported. Results do not establish population effects, hiring outcomes, interview success, offer success, or a causal improvement attributable only to JobPilot.
